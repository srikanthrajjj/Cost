01 / Real Cost Breakdown

How Much Does a Roof Replacement Actually Cost?

Most homeowners spend $8,600 to $24,700 on a full roof replacement, with the typical job landing around $16,650. That range is wide because it's driven by five things: total square footage, roof pitch, material choice, how many layers need to be torn off, and your local labor market.

A simple way to think about it: labor makes up 40–60% of the bill, materials cover another 30–40%, and the rest goes to permits, disposal, and any structural repairs the crew finds once the old roof comes off. That last category is exactly why a written, itemized quote matters more than the bottom-line number.

Pro Tip

Ask every contractor to price your job in "squares" (100 sq ft units), not just a flat total. It's the industry-standard unit and it's the fastest way to compare two quotes apples-to-apples.

03 / What Moves Your Price

Seven Factors That Change Your Quote

  • Roof size — priced per square (100 sq ft); a 2,500 sq ft roof footprint runs roughly 25–30 squares once pitch is factored in.
  • Roof pitch — steep roofs need safety equipment and slow the crew down, adding 10–30% to labor.
  • Layers to remove — tearing off two existing layers costs more than one, both in labor and disposal fees.
  • Deck condition — rotted plywood found during tear-off is billed per sheet, usually $50–$80 each.
  • Roof complexity — valleys, dormers, skylights, and chimneys all add flashing and cutting time.
  • Accessibility — a roof with poor truck/ladder access costs more to stage material for.
  • Local labor rates — the same job can vary 20–40% between metro and rural markets.
Field Note

If your quote seems unusually low, check whether it accounts for tear-off disposal fees separately — some contractors omit this line item to win the bid, then add it back as a "surprise" once work starts.

04 / The Replacement Process

What Actually Happens, Start to Finish

A full tear-off-and-replace on an average home takes 3–5 working days. Here's the sequence a licensed crew follows:

  1. Inspection & written estimate

    The contractor walks the roof, checks the deck and ventilation from the attic, and gives you an itemized scope — not just a number.

  2. Permit & material order

    Your contractor files for the permit and orders shingles, underlayment, and flashing sized to your exact roof measurements.

  3. Tear-off & deck inspection

    Old roofing comes off down to bare decking. This is when hidden rot or water damage gets found — and quoted separately.

  4. Underlayment & flashing

    A water-resistant barrier goes down first, then new flashing around every chimney, vent, and valley.

  5. New roofing installation

    Shingles, panels, or tiles go on to manufacturer spec — correct nailing pattern and exposure matter more than speed here.

  6. Cleanup & final inspection

    A magnetic sweep clears stray nails, and you walk the job together against the original written scope before final payment.

05 / Permits & Insurance

Permits and Insurance Coverage

Most jurisdictions require a permit for a full roof replacement, and a licensed contractor will usually handle the paperwork as part of the job — permit costs typically run $100 to $500 depending on your municipality.

Insurance: homeowner's policies generally cover sudden, storm-related damage — hail, wind, fire — but not gradual wear or age. If you're filing a claim, document damage with photos before any repair work begins, and get an adjuster's assessment alongside your contractor's.

Pro Tip

Ask to see the permit pulled in your name before work starts. An unpermitted roof replacement can complicate a home sale or insurance claim later, even if the workmanship is fine.

06 / Contractor Red Flags

Red Flags to Catch Before You Sign

These are the most common ways a roofing quote quietly costs you more than it should:

Red Flag

No itemized scope. A single lump-sum number with no material, labor, or disposal breakdown makes it impossible to compare quotes or know what you're actually paying for.

Red Flag

Large upfront deposit. Reputable contractors typically ask for 10–30% down, not 50%+ before any work begins.

Red Flag

No mention of permits. If the quote is silent on who's pulling the permit, ask directly — it should be the contractor, not you.

Red Flag

Pressure to sign today. "Storm chaser" crews that push same-day signatures after a weather event are the single biggest source of roofing complaints nationally.

Get at least three written quotes from licensed, insured contractors before deciding — it's the single most effective way to spot both overpricing and missing scope.

08 / FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does roof replacement cost in 2026?

Most homeowners pay between $8,600 and $24,700 for a full roof replacement, with a national average around $16,650. The exact price depends on roof size, pitch, material, and local labor rates.

What is the cheapest roofing material?

Asphalt shingles are the least expensive option, typically running $3.50 to $5.50 per square foot installed, and remain the most common choice in the U.S.

How long does a roof replacement take?

A typical asphalt shingle replacement takes 3 to 5 days. Metal, tile, or slate roofs, and steep or complex rooflines, can extend the timeline to one or two weeks.

Does homeowners insurance cover roof replacement?

Insurance typically covers sudden damage from storms, hail, or fire, but not gradual wear, age-related deterioration, or lack of maintenance. Check your policy's specific exclusions.

Should I repair or replace my roof?

Repair usually makes sense for damage covering less than 30% of the roof on a structure under 15 years old. Replacement is typically more cost-effective once damage is widespread or the roof is near the end of its material lifespan.

How many roofing quotes should I get?

Get at least three written quotes from licensed, insured contractors. This surfaces the fair price range for your area and makes it easier to spot a quote that is missing scope or padded with extras.
